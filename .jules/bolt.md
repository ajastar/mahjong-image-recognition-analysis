## 2024-05-13 - [Agari Domain Constraint Fast-Fail]
**Learning:** Checking every tile index (0-33) as a potential pair candidate for a standard Mahjong hand involves many redundant evaluations. A mathematically sound domain constraint applies: a standard winning hand consists of 4 sets of 3 and 1 pair of 2. Therefore, the sum of tiles in the suit containing the pair MUST be 2 (mod 3), and all other suits MUST sum to 0 (mod 3). Using modulo logic to enforce this fast-fails invalid hands and constrains the search space for the pair to a single suit.
**Action:** When evaluating discrete permutations with mathematical constraints (like the 3-and-2 structure of Mahjong hands), compute and check the modular sums early to reject invalid inputs and limit the search space, which produces significant speedups.
