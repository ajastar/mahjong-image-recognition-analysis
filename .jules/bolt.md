## 2026-07-12 - Unrolling sum() Generators in Hot Paths
**Learning:** In CPython, evaluating generator comprehensions inside `sum()` (e.g., `sum(1 for x in obj)`) or calling `sum()` on a slice/list introduces significant function call and iteration overhead in hot paths. The benchmark showed that using explicit `for` loops with an early `break` for boolean checks is ~5-15x faster than `sum()` over generators, and unrolled boolean additions (e.g., `(a > 0) + (b > 0)`) are ~6x faster than `sum()`. Replacing `sum()` on fixed-length arrays with explicit additions (e.g., `arr[0] + arr[1] + arr[2]`) avoids function overhead completely.
**Action:** Always replace `sum()` with generator expressions in frequently called evaluation or simulation loops with explicit loops (using early breaks if possible) or manual additions for small, fixed-domain domains (like suits or tile indices) to maximize CPython execution speed.
